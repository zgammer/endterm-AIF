Chapter VIIIThe Apprentice Learns

In the old way, a person writes every rule: if the temperature is above 80, ring the alarm. But real data are noisy, thresholds overlap and patterns bend. So the apprentice does something different. He studies examples and outcomes, and a learning algorithm finds the rules itself. We write it ŷ = fθ(x): features x go in (say temperature and vibration), learned parameters θ shape the mapping f, and a prediction ŷ comes out, a number or a class.

Chapter VIIIThe Workshop Routine

Every learning project follows five steps: collect labeled examples, split them into training and test sets, fit the model, evaluate it on unseen data, then deploy it on new cases. One law must never be broken: test information must not leak into fitting, or the grade means nothing.

The kind of feedback the teacher gives defines three paradigms:

Chapter VIIINumbers and Labels

Regression predicts a continuous number such as energy demand or a price. Classification predicts a class or its probability, such as failure or fraud. The simplest regression draws a line: ŷ = wx + b. If consumption = 5 × temperature + 100, then at 30°C we predict 250 kWh.

How do we pick the line? By minimizing the average squared error:

MSE = (1/n) Σi (yi − ŷi)²

Squaring punishes big misses: an error of 10 costs 100, an error of 2 only 4. For regression we judge models by MAE, RMSE and R²; for classification by precision, recall and F1.

Fit the five readings (15, 174), (20, 201), (25, 224), (30, 251), (35, 278) and you get a slope near 5.16 and an intercept near 96.6, so 32°C predicts about 262 kWh.

A probability is not yet a decision. We predict failure when P(failure | x) ≥ τ. The threshold τ is a policy, not a law of the model. The default 0.5 is not always best: when failures are costly, τ may be 0.2 or lower, and a lower τ catches more real failures.

It echoes the last chapter: the cost-based threshold 2,000 / 10,000 = 0.20 is exactly such a τ.

Chapter VIIITrees and Forests

A decision tree is a ladder of if–then questions: is the temperature above 70°C? Is the vibration above 3? Its rules can be read aloud, it bends around nonlinear patterns and needs little preprocessing. But a deep tree may overfit, and a small change in the data can grow a different tree. A random forest plants many trees: each sees a bootstrap sample of the rows and a random subset of features, and the forest votes. Averaging many diverse trees usually reduces variance, at the cost of being harder to explain.

Chapter VIIIFinding Groups

Sometimes no one has labeled the data. K-Means finds groups by similarity in four moves: choose K centroids; assign each point to its nearest centroid; move each centroid to the mean of its points; repeat until nothing changes. It minimizes the within-cluster squared distance:

J = Σk Σx∈Ck ‖x − μk‖²

Features with big units (spending in dollars next to purchase counts) would dominate the distance, so we scale them first. Cluster numbers are only names: cluster 2 is not “better” than cluster 0. The algorithm finds the groups, but people must decide what they mean.

X_scaled = StandardScaler().fit_transform(X)
km = KMeans(n_clusters=3, n_init=10, random_state=42)
labels = km.fit_predict(X_scaled)

Chapter VIIIIs the Model Any Good?

Suppose only 2% of observations are failures. A lazy model that always says “normal” scores 98% accuracy and finds 0% of failures. So we also measure recall = TP / (TP + FN), the share of real failures caught, and precision = TP / (TP + FP), the share of alarms that are real. F1 = 2PR / (P + R) balances the two, though not the actual costs. Model A (accuracy 95%, recall 60%) may be worse than Model B (accuracy 92%, recall 90%) when missed failures are dangerous.

Evaluate honestly: learn on the train split, tune on validation, report on test, then check calibration, since a 70% prediction should come true about 70% of the time. Beware overfitting, data leakage, distribution shift and miscalibration. For rational decisions, trustworthy probabilities can matter more than raw accuracy.

Chapter VIIIThe Trial of the Apprentice

1. Only 2% of cases are failures. A model that always predicts “normal” has what accuracy and failure recall?

It is right on all normal cases, and it never detects a failure.
Accuracy = 98%, recall = 0 / (number of failures) = 0%. Accuracy hides the problem.

2. K-Means is which kind of learning?

Does K-Means receive any target answers?
K-Means gets no labels. It discovers structure, and people interpret the clusters.

3. A logistic model outputs P(failure) = 0.30. Failures are very costly. What fits best?

The threshold is a policy that depends on costs.
τ is a decision policy. When failure is costly, τ may be 0.2 or lower, and 0.30 ≥ 0.2 triggers an alert.
